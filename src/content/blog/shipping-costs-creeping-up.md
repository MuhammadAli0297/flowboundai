Nobody decides to overpay for shipping. It happens a lane at a time: a carrier raises rates and nobody
re-shops the route, a rush order goes out express because there wasn't time to compare options, a lane that
made sense a year ago quietly stops being the cheapest one. None of it looks like a problem in the moment.
Add it up over a quarter and it's real money.

## The creep, not the spike

Most small teams pick a carrier and a shipping method per lane once, usually when the account first gets
set up, and rarely revisit it unless something goes wrong. Carriers, meanwhile, adjust rates constantly,
and the cheapest option for a given lane six months ago often isn't the cheapest option today. Nobody's
being careless. There just isn't time to re-shop every lane by hand on a regular basis.

## Three places shipping costs hide

- A lane that's been on the same carrier long enough that nobody remembers to check the alternatives.
- Rush shipping used to cover for a late reorder, which shows up as a shipping cost but is really an
  inventory planning problem.
- Small, frequent orders to the same destination that would be cheaper consolidated, if anyone had the
  time to notice the pattern.

## The habit that catches it early

Pick a handful of your highest-volume lanes and re-shop them against current rates once a quarter. It
won't catch everything, but it catches the worst of the creep, and it's a habit that costs an afternoon and
usually pays for itself the same day you do it.
