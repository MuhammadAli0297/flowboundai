The wrong carrier for a shipping lane almost never fails in a way you'd notice. The package still arrives.
The customer still gets their order. It just costs a bit more than it should have, or takes a day longer
than the better option would have, and because nothing actually went wrong, nobody goes back to check
whether a cheaper or faster option existed.

## A wrong choice that never looks wrong

Most carrier decisions get made once, when an account or a lane first gets set up, and then run on autopilot.
That's a reasonable way to start. It stops being reasonable once rates shift and a once-competitive carrier
quietly becomes the expensive option for that route, with nothing in the day-to-day experience signaling
that a change happened.

## What actually varies lane by lane

Carriers aren't uniformly cheap or expensive. One carrier can be the best option for a short regional lane
and a mediocre one for a cross-country shipment, because rates are built around each carrier's own network
and hub locations. A single "preferred carrier" for every lane almost guarantees you're overpaying on some
of them.

## Why nobody catches it in the moment

Catching this requires comparing the lane against every viable alternative, at current rates, which is
exactly the kind of task that's easy to justify skipping when there's no obvious problem prompting it. The
cost isn't a single bad decision. It's dozens of slightly-suboptimal ones a month, quietly compounding.
