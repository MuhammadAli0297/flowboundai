Most small distributors track how fast they respond to a customer message. Fewer track whether that first
response actually solved the problem, and that second number matters more. A fast reply that starts with "let
me look into that and get back to you" hasn't resolved anything, it's just started a longer conversation with
a quicker opening line. First contact resolution measures the thing response time doesn't: whether the
customer's question got a real, final answer the first time they asked.

## What first contact resolution actually measures

First contact resolution, or FCR, is the percentage of customer inquiries fully resolved during the first
interaction, no follow-up message, no escalation, no "checking on this and will circle back." The formula is
straightforward: resolved-on-first-contact cases divided by total cases, times 100.

The reason it's a better metric than response time for a wholesale operation specifically: your customers
aren't asking generic questions, they're asking questions your own systems already have the answer to. "Where's
my order," "can I get a partial shipment," "why was I charged a different price than last time," these all
have a factual, look-it-up answer sitting in your order, shipment, or account data. A slow first response to a
question like that is a minor annoyance. A fast first response that still requires a second message because
nobody actually pulled the real answer is the more expensive failure, since it costs the same round trip
either way, just with a worse first impression.

## Why FCR matters more for wholesale than it does for general support

General customer support benchmarks put a solid first contact resolution rate around 70 to 75 percent, with
80 percent or higher considered elite. Those numbers come mostly from consumer call centers answering a wide
range of unpredictable questions. A wholesale distributor's support volume looks different: it's
concentrated in a small number of repeatable question types, order status, backorder timing, return
eligibility, pricing on an account, which means FCR has more headroom to be higher, not less, if the answer
is actually sitting somewhere accessible the moment the question comes in.

That concentration is also why a low FCR rate in wholesale support is usually a data problem, not a training
problem. The rep isn't unable to answer, they're waiting on someone else, a warehouse check, a shipping
carrier's tracking page, a look-up in a separate order system, before they can give a real answer. Every one
of those lookups turns what should be a first-contact resolution into a "let me get back to you," and the
customer experiences that as slow service even when the actual response time was fast.

## Where wholesale FCR usually breaks

**Order status questions answered without live data.** [Order tracking is fundamentally a data problem, not
a communication one](/blog/wheres-my-order-is-a-supply-chain-question/): if the rep answering the question
doesn't have the current shipment status in front of them, "let me check and follow up" is the only honest
answer they can give, and that's an FCR miss by definition, no matter how quickly they said it.

**Return and claim requests that need cross-checking.** [A return request actually needs the original order
and shipment record checked against it](/blog/what-a-return-request-actually-needs/) before anyone can say
yes or no. If that check requires pulling a different system than the one the support conversation is
happening in, the answer waits, and so does resolution.

**The same handful of questions asked over and over.** Teams that field [the same three questions every
day](/blog/same-three-questions-your-team-answers-daily/) often already know the answers cold, the delay
isn't uncertainty, it's the mechanical time spent looking each one up individually instead of having it
surface automatically alongside the customer's message.

**Backorder timing that depends on a supplier's current lead time, not a stored promise date.** If a
customer asks when a [backordered item](/blog/backordered-item-customer-communication/) will actually ship
and the honest answer requires checking a supplier's latest lead time rather than repeating whatever date was
quoted weeks ago, that lookup is another first-contact miss waiting to happen.

## Raising FCR without adding headcount

The instinct when FCR is low is to hire more support staff, but more people doing the same manual lookups
just does the same slow process in parallel, it doesn't fix the underlying gap between where a question
arrives and where its answer lives. The actual fix is closing that gap: making sure whoever, or whatever,
answers a customer's first message already has the order, shipment, inventory, and account data in front of
them, instead of needing to go ask someone else first.

That's a workflow and data-access problem more than a staffing one. A support rep who can see live order and
shipment status inside the same conversation they're having with the customer resolves far more on first
contact than one who has to switch systems, or wait on a colleague, to get the same information. The
question isn't "how many people do we need," it's "how many of our answers require a second lookup that
shouldn't."

## FAQ

**What is first contact resolution?**
The percentage of customer inquiries fully resolved during the first interaction, with no follow-up message
or escalation needed. It's calculated as resolved-on-first-contact cases divided by total cases, times 100.

**Why is first contact resolution a better metric than response time?**
Response time only measures how fast you replied, not whether the reply actually solved the problem. A fast
"let me check and get back to you" still requires a second interaction, which is the exact failure FCR is
designed to catch, the same reason [order tracking has to be treated as a data problem before a
communication one](/blog/wheres-my-order-is-a-supply-chain-question/).

**What's a good first contact resolution rate?**
General support benchmarks put a solid rate around 70 to 75 percent, with 80 percent or higher considered
elite. Wholesale support has more room to exceed that, since its question types are more repeatable and
concentrated than general consumer support, provided [inventory and shipment data](/inventory-tracking/) is
actually accessible at the point the question comes in.

**Why does first contact resolution break down for backordered items specifically?**
Because an honest answer about when a [backordered item](/blog/backordered-item-customer-communication/)
will ship depends on a supplier's current lead time, not a promise date quoted weeks earlier, and pulling
that current number often means a lookup outside the conversation itself.

**Does raising first contact resolution require more support staff?**
Not usually. Low FCR is typically a data-access problem, the rep or agent answering doesn't have order,
shipment, or account data in front of them yet, not a staffing shortage, which is why [Ask
Flowbound](/ask-flowbound/) focuses on closing that access gap rather than adding more people doing the same
manual lookups in parallel.
