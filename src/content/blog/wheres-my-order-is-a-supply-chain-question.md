A "where's my order" message lands in the support inbox and gets treated like a customer service problem:
someone replies, apologizes for the wait, and goes looking for an answer. But the question itself has
nothing to do with customer service. It's a question about a shipment's actual location, and the person
answering it usually doesn't have a better view of that than the customer asking it.

## The ticket that's never really about the ticket

Support tickets about order status aren't really about the order. They're a symptom of a gap between what
a customer can see and what the business actually knows. If that gap didn't exist, the answer would take ten
seconds instead of a search across three tabs and a message to whoever handles shipping.

## Why the answer is harder to find than it should be

Most small teams don't have one place that shows order status, shipment tracking, and inventory together.
The order lives in one system, the tracking number lives in an email from the carrier, and inventory lives
somewhere else entirely. Answering a single "where's my order" question means checking all three, and doing
it fast enough that the customer doesn't send a second message before the first one's answered.

## What a good answer actually requires

A real answer, not a guess or a canned "it's on its way," requires connecting the order to the actual
shipment record in real time. That's a data problem before it's a customer service problem. Fixing the
support experience means fixing the visibility gap underneath it, not writing a faster reply.

## Answering it before your team has to

The best version of this doesn't involve your team at all. [Customer Service](/customer-service) puts your
live order, shipment, and inventory data behind an agent that answers "where's my order" directly, pulled
from the actual shipment record, so it's answered before it ever needs a person to go looking.
