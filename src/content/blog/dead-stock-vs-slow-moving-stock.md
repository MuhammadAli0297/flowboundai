Walk through most small warehouses and you'll find the same thing: a corner of SKUs that haven't moved in
months, all lumped together under one label, "slow stuff." Some of that inventory is genuinely dead, it's
never selling again and the only question is how much you recover on it. The rest is just slow, and treating
it like the first group means you stop reordering something that would have sold fine with a little more
patience.

## Two problems that look identical on a shelf

Dead stock and slow-moving stock look the same sitting on a shelf: quiet, dusty, not turning over. The
difference only shows up when you look at the trend behind the number, not the number itself. A SKU that
sold five units a month for a year and then stopped is dead. A SKU that's always sold five units a month,
slowly and steadily, was never dead to begin with.

## What actually separates them

The real test is velocity over time, not velocity at a glance. Compare the last three months of sales
against the twelve before that. A SKU with a flat, ongoing trickle of sales is slow-moving and probably
fine to keep at a lower reorder quantity. A SKU whose trickle stopped entirely, with no seasonal reason for
it, is dead, and the sooner you write it off and free the cash, the better.

## Why the wrong label costs you either way

Call a slow mover dead, and you stop reordering it right before a customer who actually wants it shows up.
Call dead stock slow, and it sits taking up space and tying up cash for another six months on the theory
that it'll eventually move. Both mistakes are expensive. Both come from treating "hasn't sold much lately"
as one category instead of two very different ones.

## Watching the difference instead of guessing at it

Telling the two apart by memory works until you're tracking more than a handful of SKUs, at which point it
becomes a spreadsheet exercise nobody has time to run every month.
[Inventory Tracking](/inventory-tracking) keeps real-time visibility into what's on hand and how it's
actually trending, so the distinction between dead and slow shows up in the data instead of depending on
someone remembering which is which.
