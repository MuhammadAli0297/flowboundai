"I'd like to return this" looks like one of the simpler messages a support inbox gets. Answering it
correctly, not just quickly, requires checking several things that don't live in the same place: what was
actually ordered, what actually shipped, whether it's within the return window, and what your policy allows
for this particular case.

## The request that looks simple until you check it

A return request is really a small verification task wearing a simple sentence. Approve it without checking,
and you risk approving something outside policy. Make the customer wait while someone manually digs through
order history and shipment records, and a simple request turns into a slow, frustrating one for no reason
other than the information wasn't in one place.

## What actually has to be verified

Handling a return correctly means confirming the order exists, matches what the customer describes,
actually shipped and arrived when they say it did, and falls inside whatever return window and condition
your policy sets. Skip any one of those checks and you're either approving something you shouldn't, or
making the customer defend a request that was legitimate all along.

## Where teams get it wrong under time pressure

Under a busy queue, the checks get shortened. Someone approves a return based on the customer's word because
pulling the actual order record takes longer than they have. That's a reasonable shortcut when you're
understaffed and it's still the source of most return disputes and most quietly-too-generous approvals.

## Checking the record instead of guessing

The fix isn't asking your team to move faster through the same manual process. It's checking the actual
order and shipment record automatically, every time. [Customer Service](/customer-service) checks a return
or damage claim against the real order and shipment data and routes it correctly, so your team isn't
chasing down details before they can even start helping.
