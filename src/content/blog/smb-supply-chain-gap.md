Supply chain software tends to get built for one of two ends of the market. Enterprise ERP platforms serve
companies large enough to staff an implementation team and keep a systems integrator on retainer. Free tools
and plain spreadsheets serve businesses small enough that a single person can hold the whole operation in
their head. Most small and mid-sized businesses are neither. They're too complex for a spreadsheet and too
small for a seven-figure rollout, and almost nothing gets built specifically for that gap.

## What SAP was actually built for

SAP and platforms like it aren't wrong for what they do. They were built for organizations with dedicated
IT and operations teams, a budget for a multi-month implementation, and enough transaction volume to justify
the cost. That's a real, valuable thing to build. It's just a different customer than a business running a
few warehouses and a lean team that needs decisions made this week, not after a rollout next year.

## What happens to everyone in between

Without a tool built for their size, mid-market operators end up assembling their own patchwork: a
spreadsheet here, a supplier's own portal there, a phone call to fill in whatever neither one shows. It
works, mostly, until a lead time slips or a reorder gets missed and the gap in the patchwork is exactly
where the mistake happened. The business isn't badly run. It's running on tools sized for someone else.

## A decision engine, not a bigger dashboard

The fix isn't a smaller, cheaper version of an enterprise ERP; most of what makes those platforms heavy is
exactly what a smaller team doesn't need. It's a system built from the start for this size of business: one
that reads the data already sitting in your existing tools and turns it into a decision, with the reasoning
attached, instead of another dashboard to check. That's the gap [Flowbound](/product/) is built to close.
