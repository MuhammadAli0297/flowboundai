Ask a small business owner how a given supplier performs on quality, and the answer is usually a gut
feeling: "they're pretty good," or "we've had some issues lately." That feeling isn't wrong, exactly. It's
just an unstructured summary of a track record that already exists in the business's own data and never got
pulled together into something you could actually point to in a conversation.

## The track record that's scattered across three systems

Every return, every failed inspection, every late or short shipment is a data point about a supplier's
actual performance. The problem isn't that this information doesn't exist. It's that it lives in three
different places, a returns log, an inspection sheet, a purchase order system, and nobody has the time to
cross-reference all three every time a supplier conversation comes up.

## What a scorecard actually needs

A useful supplier scorecard doesn't need to be complicated. It needs three things tracked consistently over
time: defect rate, on-time-in-full performance, and how both are trending, not just where they stand today.
Most of that data already exists somewhere in your systems. The only thing usually missing is a habit of
pulling it into one place per supplier.

## Why this conversation usually happens too late

Without a running record, the supplier quality conversation only happens after something's gone wrong badly
enough to notice, which means you're negotiating from a position of "this just happened" instead of "here's
six months of data." The second conversation gets better results, and it only happens if the numbers were
being tracked the whole time, not assembled after the fact.
