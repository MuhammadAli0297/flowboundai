"Real-time inventory" shows up on nearly every supply chain tool's homepage, which is a good sign the phrase
has stopped meaning much. Ask what it actually guarantees, and the answers vary a lot: some systems mean a
dashboard that refreshes when you load the page, others mean a nightly sync from your point-of-sale system,
and a few genuinely mean what the words say. The gap between those is exactly where a stockout hides.

## A phrase that's stopped meaning much

The honest test for "real-time" isn't how the number looks on screen. It's how current the number actually
is the moment you need to act on it. A dashboard can look sharp and current and still be reading from a sync
job that ran eight hours ago. Nobody notices the difference until the number was wrong at the exact moment
it mattered.

## What real-time should actually mean

Real-time inventory should mean the count reflects what's actually on hand right now, including what just
sold, what just arrived, and what's already spoken for by an open order, not what the count was at the last
scheduled refresh. If a system can't answer "what changed in the last hour and why" it's not really
real-time, whatever the marketing page says.

## Where the delay usually hides

- Batch syncs that run on a schedule, hourly or nightly, instead of updating as changes happen.
- Manual entry steps between a physical count and the system reflecting it.
- Multiple systems, a POS, a spreadsheet, a supplier portal, that never fully agree with each other.

## Watching continuously instead of checking in

The fix isn't a faster refresh button. It's a system built to watch continuously in the first place, so
there's no gap between what happened and what the number shows.
